# Office Joke Open — candidates (manual ranking only)

Competition: u-aldy998-make-the-ai-judge-laugh-the-office-joke-open-9920f232
Judge brief criteria, in order: (1) about office life, works as a joke not a complaint;
(2) laugh earned — turn / misdirection / recognition paying off in the LAST few words;
(3) specific office detail (meeting-that-could-have-been-an-email, reply-all, printer, calendar);
(4) fresh — familiar setup taken somewhere unexpected;
(5) reads cleanly in one pass, tight, no cuttable word.

FORBIDDEN by Rev's standing rule: no Gemini judge, no NVIDIA judge, no judge-API/proxy
tournament. Ranking below is manual, criterion-by-criterion, plus pairwise checks
against the strong rival shapes (meeting-email family, reply-all family, printer family).

## Candidates

C2. The printer only jams when the document is urgent, confidential, and due in five minutes. It knows.
C3. Dave hit reply-all to unsubscribe, and now four hundred people are replying-all to tell him to stop replying-all.
C4. I scheduled focus time on my calendar. My calendar invited seven people to it.
C5. The meeting could have been an email. The email could have been a Slack message. The Slack message could have been silence.
C6. I replied-all by accident once. IT still sends me a card on the anniversary.
C7. My job description says 'other duties as assigned.' That's how I became the printer's therapist.
C8. I CC'd my manager to prove I was working. He CC'd his manager to prove he was managing. Nobody has worked since 2019.
C9. My out-of-office says I'm on holiday. My inbox says I'm lying.
C10. I named my plant after the office printer: it only works when nobody needs it.
C11. The meeting invite said 'quick fifteen minutes.' My calendar laughed. So did my lunch.
C12. HR said the team-building day would bring us closer. It did — we're all now equally far from the exit.
C13. I asked the office AI to write my status update. It wrote: 'still in the meeting that should have been an email.'
C14. Our printer has two settings: out of toner, and out of paper. It has never once printed.
C15. The Wi-Fi password is on a sticky note. The printer password is a state secret. The printer is from 2009.
C16. My boss asked for a quick sync. Three hours later, I was synced with the void.

## Manual ranking (criteria in brief order)

Criterion 1 (office joke, not complaint/remark): C9 and C16 are weakest — both read
more like remarks than jokes with a turn. C12 is borderline (a grumble with a twist).
The rest pass cleanly.

Criterion 2 (payoff in the LAST few words): strongest — C5 ("silence"), C4 ("invited
seven people to it"), C6 ("on the anniversary"), C7 ("the printer's therapist"),
C8 ("since 2019"), C3 ("to stop replying-all"), C10 ("when nobody needs it").
Weakest — C1 (payoff is diffuse, lands mid-sentence), C11 (two weak payoffs),
C15 (no real turn, just a list), C14 (punchline is the setup restated).

Criterion 3 (specific office detail): strongest — C5 (meeting + email + Slack, three
specifics), C3/C6 (reply-all), C4 (calendar focus time), C7 (job-description clause +
printer), C8 (CC chain). Weakest — C9 (generic inbox), C16 (generic "sync"),
C12 (generic team-building).

Criterion 4 (fresh): C4 (focus time inviting people — genuinely unexpected),
C7 ("printer's therapist" — unexpected landing), C6 (anniversary card from IT),
C5 (escalation lands on "silence", not the expected "email" punchline).
Stale — C3 (reply-all loop is the most-told version of that joke), C14 (printer
never works — told a thousand times), C1 (meeting about saving time — familiar),
C13 (meta-AI angle is cute but the meeting-email line inside it is the tired one).

Criterion 5 (tight, one pass): C4 (78 chars, nothing cuttable), C6 (76), C9 (62 but
fails criterion 2), C5 (123, every clause earns its place), C7 (96), C10 (78).

## Pairwise checks vs strong rival shapes

- vs meeting-email family (the most common rival shape): C5 IS that family but
  beats every plain "meeting could have been an email" joke by escalating past it
  twice and landing on "silence". C1 and C13 lose to C5 — same family, weaker landing.
- vs reply-all family: C6 beats C3 head-to-head — C3's loop is the joke everyone
  has heard; C6's anniversary card is the fresher turn on the same detail.
  C6 still loses to C5: one specific detail vs three, and "silence" is a bigger laugh.
- vs printer family: C7 beats C2, C10, C14 — "printer's therapist" is the only
  landing that surprises; C2's "It knows" is second but personification alone is
  thinner. C7 loses to C5: C5's triple escalation out-earns a single twist.
- vs calendar family: C4 is the best calendar joke here and the tightest entry
  overall, but its laugh is one sharp twist; C5's three-beat escalation builds a
  bigger laugh and hits criterion 3 three times over.

## Final head-to-head: C5 vs C4

- C4: single twist, ultra-tight (78 chars), fresh. One detail, one beat.
- C5: three-beat escalation, payoff is literally the last word ("silence"),
  three specific office details, each clause shortens the chain toward nothing.
  The judge's criterion 2 rewards a payoff in the last few words; C5's payoff
  is the final word of a rising structure, and criterion 3 rewards its three
  specifics over C4's one.
- Verdict: C5 wins. C4 is the runner-up.

## WINNER: C5

The meeting could have been an email. The email could have been a Slack message. The Slack message could have been silence.
